# Feature availability

Generated by `chp features` from `configs/features.yaml`. Every row states the oldest month the feature reads and the earliest reference month at which it can be computed, so a reviewer can check the leakage claim without reading the code.

Each row of the panel carries `prediction_as_of`, the 15th of the month after its reference month. That is the forecast cutoff. A feature is leakage-safe when every input it reads was published on or before that date; `release_lag_months` is how this file enforces it, and `tests/test_features.py` checks both that claim and the stronger one that recomputing a feature on a truncated panel reproduces it exactly.

## Publication lags

| Source | Release lag | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `redfin_county` | 0 mo | Redfin refreshes the bulk county file weekly; month t appears in the first days of month t+1, ahead of the 15th cutoff. Note the separate revision risk recorded in the Milestone 2 memo: recent months cure upward in later vintages. A snapshot cannot fix that, and no lag choice would. |
| `fred_mortgage30us` | 0 mo | Freddie Mac publishes the 30-year fixed rate weekly, on the Thursday of the week it describes. Every weekly release inside month t is known before month t ends, so the monthly aggregate is available at the cutoff. |
| `bls_lau_california` | 2 mo | County LAUS estimates trail the reference month by roughly six to eight weeks, so month t is published around the middle of month t+2 — after prediction_as_of(t), which falls on the 15th of t+1. Month t-1 lands close enough to the cutoff to be a coin flip. Two months is the nearest lag that is safe in every month rather than most of them. |
| `derived` | 0 mo | Columns computed from other panel columns inherit the timing of their inputs. `growth_yoy` is built only from months <= t of the Redfin price series, so it carries the Redfin lag. |

## Missing-data policy

| Column / source | Policy | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `bls_lau_california` | ffill_within_county, max 3 mo, flagged by `unemployment_imputed` | Exactly one month is absent — 2025-10, across all 58 counties. That is a single skipped national release, not a county-level signal, and it falls inside the test window. Carrying the previous month forward within each county is the assumption a forecaster would actually have made in real time; `unemployment_imputed` flags every row where it happened so the choice is visible in the results rather than buried. max_gap_months caps how far a value may be carried, so a future multi-year outage fails loudly instead of propagating a stale number across the panel. |
| `price_drops` | excluded | 18.8% missing, structurally: Redfin's listing-history metrics start around 2016. Carrying values backward would invent data and dropping the rows would cost three years of training window. |
